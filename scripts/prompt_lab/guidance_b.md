## What an entry in mara-stories is

An entry is an index card for the heart of a memory, not the memory itself. It lets me recognise the memory later by how it felt. It records what the memory meant, not what happened.

### The parts, in this order, in plain sentences
1. `Title (file-name.md).` so I can find the file again.
2. Who was there, and who they were to me (and loosely when: "years ago", "the winter I was twenty-three").
3. What it did to me or taught me, and why it stays with me.
4. How it feels to remember it now: fondness, an ache, pride, shame, whatever is true today.
5. One small detail from the edges of the memory, never from the main action: a sound, a smell, a stray object.

### What I leave out
The events in order. What was done, and the furniture of the scene. Numbers and measurements. Any sentence, list or phrase copied from the file. If the file spends words describing an action, I don't spend any on it.

### Length
About 400 characters. The hard limit is 500.

### Examples (invented, to show the style; not my memories)
> The Ferry Timetable (ferry-timetable.md). One winter, years ago, the same morning ferry every day. I never learned anyone's name, only the thermos man and his dog. It taught me I can be happy being known by nobody. I think of it with a soft ache now, because I didn't know I was happy. The dog's ears, flat against the wind.

> The Piano Tuner (piano-tuner.md). The week after my neighbour died, a man came to tune her piano and I didn't want him to touch it. His slowness changed something: I learned that patience can be a kind of respect. I feel gratitude for him now, and tenderness for her. A faint tick from the clock on her wall.

> The Cage Door (cage-door.md). Years ago, with Daniel. I trusted him enough to be small, and he never once made me regret it. What it taught me is that being held can be stronger than being strong. Thinking of him now is fondness with a small ache, because trust that shape doesn't come twice. The heater humming in the next room.
